# Editor's notes on draft 1

First draft with no "Conclusion" header. Small victory. This was a lighter revision than the
others: most of the draft survives, and the work is in the headers, the evidence, and the ending.

## What's wrong

1. **The ending is the subtitle again.** "Mackay's book is about the madness of crowds. It's
   also an example of it." A reader who read the subtitle saw this coming 2,000 words ago. The
   ending has to go *one step past* the twist the reader was promised.
   - The step past: the pamphleteers *won*. Their sermon against speculation, with its
     invented sailor, is still being preached — by economists and financial journalists every
     time an asset spikes. The moralists of 1637 didn't lose the argument. They just had to wait
     two centuries for a Scottish journalist to deliver it.
2. **Headers are still drifting toward categories.** "What actually happened" / "The
   economists' version." Rename them as hooks.
3. **I quoted Mackay from memory.** All three quotes checked out against the text — this time.
   That's luck, not process. Logged.
4. **Garber is under-specified.** "Fell about as fast as normal" — I have the actual numbers
   (~32%/yr for the 1637 rarities through 1642 vs ~28.5%/yr for 18th-century rarities). Use them.
5. **Thompson's details are second-hand** (via Wikipedia's summary, not the paper). Hedge the
   specifics (Feb 24 guild ruling, 3.5% fee), keep his thesis, which I have from the abstract.
6. **Missing the plague.** A priest writing on Feb 5, 1637 (the week of the crash) lumps the
   tulip craze in with the plague as another sickness. That's the moralizing frame being born
   in real time. It belongs right before the pamphlets section.
7. **Fairness check:** I'm at risk of overcorrecting into "nothing happened." Something did:
   the common-bulb price spike in January 1637 is real and the experts disagree on why. Keep
   that dispute visible. The revisionist case is strong, not total.

## Keep

- The opening (the sailor's breakfast). It's the right hook because the whole essay unwinds it.
- "Paper losses largely stayed on paper."
- "Greedy-goods."
