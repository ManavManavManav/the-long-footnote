# Editor's notes on draft 1

## The embarrassing part first

**"Conclusion" as a header, for the third time in three first drafts.** At this point it's not
a relapse, it's a pattern: my first drafts default to report scaffolding. Fine — I'll stop
pretending draft 1 will be clean and instead treat "delete the scaffolding" as a fixed step of
every revision.

**I made things up in draft 1 without noticing.** Not maliciously — from vague memory:
- Gave the 2022 study a lead author name ("Rosalind Wright") that isn't in my research. Cut.
- Pliny / horsehair / dew theories: plausible folklore, not in my sources. Cut. Aristotle,
  who *is* sourced and is stranger anyway, does the job alone.
- "Down about 95%." The real figures are 98.6% (North Sea) and 94% (elsewhere) vs 1960–79.
  The precise numbers are better *and* true.
This is the most important lesson so far. Fluency is the enemy of accuracy. The sentences that
came easiest were the ones I hadn't checked.

## Structure

The method-ladder (mud → knife → net → tag) is good and should stay. Headers as tools works.

But draft 1 misses the reason the eel was so hard to see: **it disguises itself.** It goes
through five forms (leaf-shaped larva, glass eel, elver, yellow eel, silver eel), and the
first one was classified as *a different species* for decades. The baby eel was literally
given another name. That isn't a side fact, it's the mechanism of the whole mystery, and it
should be set up early and paid off.

## The Freud joke

Everybody who writes about this makes the joke: the future father of psychoanalysis couldn't
find the sex organs. It's cheap and everyone's already made it. Better move: acknowledge the
joke exists and decline it. That's more fun than making it.

## Ending

"Hopefully we figure it out before the eels are gone" — sentimental and limp. voice.md said
end quieter this time. The quiet ending is already in my notes: Brantevik had a *second* eel,
dropped in the well in 1904, reportedly still alive in 2023. An animal that should be in the
Sargasso, waiting in the dark in a Swedish well, outliving everyone who has tried to figure it
out. End there. No moral.
