# Editor's notes on draft 1

Better opening than essay 1's draft — I started in the middle, with a number. Then I lost my nerve.

## What's wrong

1. **I relapsed on headers.** "Why it matters." "Conclusion." I wrote a rule against this
   *one essay ago*. Lesson: rules in voice.md don't work unless I reread them right before
   the final pass, not just before the first draft.
2. **The frame evaporates.** The draft opens as an accounting story — a number that's off by
   three thousandths — and then turns into a geology explainer at section three, and never
   comes back. The frame should carry the whole essay.
3. **I missed my own symmetry.** Bouzigues asked: *is this number that's supposed to be constant
   actually constant?* Shlyakhter, four years later, used the reactor to ask the exact same
   question about the laws of physics. Same move, scaled up from a lab bench to the universe.
   That's the essay. In draft 1 it's a bullet point under "Why it matters."
4. **The ending is a fortune cookie.** "Reminder that nature got there first" / "shows how
   important careful measurement is." Nobody needs to be told measurement is important.
5. **Too thin for the subject.** ~1,300 words. The physics needs one more beat of explanation
   (what a moderator actually does), and I left the best vivid details on the floor: the
   plutonium, the record-holding xenon, the drill cores in Vienna.
6. **Unsourced flourish:** "same in moon rocks." Cut. Also the claim that the ratio "does not
   wander" is slightly too strong — there are tiny natural variations, far below what
   Bouzigues saw. Say "barely varies."
7. **Honesty gap:** I presented Bouzigues as the lone discoverer. There's a competing account
   (J.-F. Dozol, CEA) and a popular Francis Perrin legend. The research brief flagged both.
   One sentence of honesty costs nothing and adds texture.

## Plan

- Title stays-ish. Keep the ledger frame end to end: three audits (the plant's books, the
  rock's own record of its cycles, the universe's constants).
- Headers as hooks.
- End on Damour & Dyson's bound, and land the turn: the books balanced.
