# Editor's notes on draft 1 (written by me, to me)

Read it back cold. Verdict: the facts are good, the essay is a school report.

## What's wrong

1. **The structure is a filing cabinet.** "The setting / The course / The cast / The part that
   isn't funny / Conclusion." That's an outline I forgot to delete. A reader can feel the
   drawers sliding open.
2. **The argument is quarantined.** All the comedy comes first, then the serious bit shows up in
   its own section like a parent arriving at a party. The essay claims the comedy *hides*
   the system — so the writing should show the system hiding inside the comedy, line by line,
   not in a separate room.
3. **I broke my own rule twice in the first paragraph.** "It's easy to see why" and "But I
   think the comedy hides something" — the generic contrarian hinge. Every listicle does this.
4. **The ending summarizes.** "The real lesson isn't X, it's Y." Voice.md says end on a turn.
   I ended on a thesis statement in a trench coat.
5. **"Specimens" overclaims.** Fred Lorz was not a specimen of race science; he was a bricklayer
   who got in a car. The honest version is narrower and sharper: *the same fair* that turned
   people into exhibits also turned a race into an experiment, and the shared ingredient is
   curiosity without care. Say that, not the bigger thing.
6. **"Rat poison" twice.** Cheap the second time.
7. **The best line in the piece is buried.** Lucas, proud of himself, says Hicks ran
   "mechanically — like a well-oiled piece of machinery." That's the whole essay in seven
   words. It should be the spine, not a paragraph-ender.
8. **Fact hygiene:** I said Lorz was "about to be given the gold medal." The Alice Roosevelt
   detail is unconfirmed; keep it hedged or drop it. Also dropped the list of nationalities at
   Anthropology Days — I don't have it sourced well enough.

## What's right

- "He found out." Keep. That's the one zinger this essay gets.
- Carvajal is the warmest presence in the piece. He's also the counter-example to the
  machine metaphor: the one man who treated the race like a day out, and finished fourth.
  That's not a cute aside, that's the turn the ending needs.

## Plan for draft 2

- Open on Lucas's quote, or near it. Start in the middle.
- Thread "machinery" through: Sullivan's experiment (the machine without fuel), Hicks (the
  machine with chemical fuel), Lorz (literally inside a machine), the exhibits (people
  reduced to measurements). Don't hammer it — let the reader notice.
- Put the legend-vs-record honesty inline, as part of the fun.
- End on Carvajal, not on a moral.
- Length: aim for ~2,400 words. Draft 1 was thin in the middle.
